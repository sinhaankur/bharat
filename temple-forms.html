Side elevation · c. 1030 CE

Nagara: Kandariya Mahadeva, Khajuraho

The northern form. The tower over the sanctum curves inward as it rises, and the halls step up toward it like foothills to a peak.

Nagara curvilinear shikhara clustered with 84 miniature towers (Mount Kailasa). ASI: 30.5 m long ≈ 30.5 m high, 20 m wide, on a ~3 m platform.

Source: ASI; UNESCO; Britannica

Front elevation · completed 1010 CE (Rajaraja Chola I)

Dravida: Brihadeeswarar (Peruvudaiyar), Thanjavur

The southern form. A square sanctum wall carries a pyramid of diminishing storeys, closed by a neck and a single capstone.

Tallest vimana of its age: a 13-storey stone pyramid ~66 m over the sanctum, octagonal griva + monolithic ~80-tonne capstone. Its gopurams are lower than the vimana here (unusual); most Dravidian temples have gopurams taller than the vimana.

Source: ASI; UNESCO Great Living Chola Temples

Side elevation · 13th c. CE (Narasimhadeva I)

Kalinga: Konark Sun Temple, Odisha

The Odishan form, here built as the chariot of the Sun: a platform on wheels, a hall with a stepped roof, and a tower that has fallen.

Chariot of Surya: 24 wheels (12 pairs = months, each an 8-spoke sundial) drawn by 7 horses (7 days / metres of prosody). Surviving jagamohana 30 m sq × ~30 m; the ~70 m deul collapsed.

Source: ASI; UNESCO

Elevation · 3rd c. BCE (Ashoka), enlarged 2nd–1st c. BCE

Stupa: Great Stupa (Stupa 1), Sanchi

A solid dome over a relic, circled on foot. The railing and gateways came after the dome.

Solid hemispherical anda 36.6 m across, 16.46 m high (dome only). Squared harmika + triple chhatra on the axis; four toranas at the cardinal points.

Source: ASI; UNESCO

Plan and shrine elevation · 8th c. CE (Lalitaditya Muktapida)

Kashmiri: Martand Sun Temple, Anantnag

A shrine set in a colonnaded court, with the steep peaked roof and trefoil arch that mark the Kashmiri style.

Square shrine on a high plinth inside a colonnaded courtyard of ~84 fluted columns; the Kashmiri signature is the steep PEAKED (pyramidal) roof and trefoil arches. Ruined; proportions from surviving plan.

Source: ASI

Plan · begun c. 1437 CE

Chaumukha: Chaumukha (Adinatha) temple, Ranakpur

A four-faced image at the centre, open to four doorways, in a forest of pillars and domes.

Light marble; 1,444 unique pillars, 29 pillared halls, 80 domes over a ~48,000 sq ft base. Four doorways to a four-faced Adinatha — a symmetrical chaumukha plan.

Source: Rajasthan Tourism; ASI

Plan · 5th–8th c. CE

Rock-cut: Great Cave (Cave 1), Elephanta

Not built but excavated. The hall, its pillars and its sculpture are one piece of basalt.

A pillared hall ~39 m across excavated into basalt; pillars are left standing, not placed. The back wall carries the ~5.5 m three-headed Trimurti Shiva — sculpture and structure are one stone.

Source: ASI; UNESCO

Blue figures are measurements from the atlas file. Everything else in a drawing is schematic: the order and names of the parts are standard, but profiles, ornament and any dimension marked indicative are not measured. Where the file gives no metres (Martand, Ranakpur), the drawing is a labelled plan with counts only. The Brihadisvara height is given as 60 to 66 m across sources; the file value is used.
