Population Control, Marauder Style

History's 100 deadliest events — wars, oppressions, failed states, despots, colonial and civil wars — sized by the number of people killed and, where the span was under 40 years, by the share of all humanity that died. This is an interactive rebuild of Bill Marsh's 2011 New York Times graphic, which visualised the death-toll estimates catalogued by Matthew White. Circles are coloured by location and grouped into the category bands; circle area shows the scale of deaths. Switch between the timeline and a world map that plots each event where it happened. Events that struck the Indian subcontinent are outlined in red.

If you have lived until now, your grandparents survived all of this.

Every name on this chart is someone's ancestor's worst year. That we're here to read it is not nothing. The record is grim — and, measured against all of us alive, slowly getting less so.
