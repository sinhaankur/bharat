The long story · 5,000 years, one timeline

Five thousand years, one spine

Five thousand years of India on one spine — each era read on five facets (language, script, people, rulers, heritage), with the evidence behind every fact open to interrogation.

How we knowBuilt from peer-reviewed ancient-DNA papers (Narasimhan & Shinde 2019), Ashoka's own edicts, and paleography. Each fact carries an evidence tier and a 'shaped by the winner' check; contested points (e.g. the Aryan-migration narrative) are flagged, not resolved.

~65,000–7000 BCE

Deep prehistory — the substrate

LanguageAncestral languages, unknown

No records. The deep population — the AASI (Ancient Ancestral South Indians) — is the substrate every later group mixed into.

ScriptNone — pre-literate

PeopleAASI hunter-gatherers established

A deeply divergent lineage, distantly related to the Andamanese; the foundational layer of South Asian ancestry.

Evidence: inference

Recorded by: modern population geneticists (inferred, not directly sampled)

Shaped by: none ancient — but a POLITICALLY charged topic today

How we know: No ancient AASI genome has been sequenced yet; AASI is a statistical source-population inferred in every model of South Asian ancestry (Narasimhan/Reich). So: strong consensus, but a reconstruction, not a body in a lab.

RulersBand / forager societies

No states.

HeritageRock art (e.g. Bhimbetka), microliths established

Some of the oldest human traces in the subcontinent.

Evidence: primary

Recorded by: the people themselves (paintings, tools)

Shaped by: none — no one to flatter

How we know: Physical remains you can still see at Bhimbetka; dating by stratigraphy and style. About as unmediated as evidence gets — nobody's ‘winner’ curated it.

  • Homo sapiens × Neanderthal admixture (~60–40 kya)
  • AASI lineage established as the substrate
The DNA record

~2600–1900 BCE

Indus (Harappan) Civilisation

LanguageUnknown (‘Harappan’)

We cannot name the language family — the script is undeciphered. Some argue an early Dravidian; unproven.

ScriptIndus script — UNDECIPHERED contested

Short seal-inscriptions; never read, and not even confirmed to be full writing. A minority view makes it Brahmi's ancestor — separated by a ~1500-year gap.

Evidence: primary

Recorded by: the Harappans (on seals)

Shaped by: MODERN politics — nationalist vs colonial readings

How we know: The marks are physically real and primary; but ‘what they SAY’ is unknown — undeciphered. Claims that it's early Dravidian, or the parent of Brahmi, are contested and often carry a modern political charge. Here the primary object is solid but every INTERPRETATION is a hypothesis.

PeopleIranian-farmer-related + AASI established

The one sequenced IVC genome (Rakhigarhi) was exactly this mix — with NO Steppe and NO Anatolian-farmer ancestry.

Evidence: peer reviewed

Recorded by: Shinde et al. 2019 (Cell) — the Rakhigarhi genome

Shaped by: n=1, so read with care; the finding itself is not ‘shaped’

How we know: A single ancient genome, sequenced and peer-reviewed. Powerful and hard to spin — but it IS one individual, and the authors say so. Strong evidence, honestly bounded.

RulersNo evidence of kings/armies debated

Remarkably uniform cities (Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira) with no palaces or grand tombs — governance still debated.

Evidence: primary

Recorded by: the archaeological record (absence of palaces/royal tombs)

Shaped by: none ancient — but ‘absence of evidence’ is slippery

How we know: We infer ‘no kings’ from what's MISSING — no palaces, no royal graves, no army art. That's a reasonable read of primary excavation, but an argument from silence: a very different footing from a genome or an inscription.

HeritagePlanned cities, the Great Bath, standardised weights, drainage

Among the most sophisticated urban planning of the Bronze Age.

  • Peak urban phase ~2600–1900 BCE
  • Gradual decline ~1900 BCE (climate/river shifts, not a proven invasion)

~1500–500 BCE

The Vedic period

LanguageVedic Sanskrit (Indo-Aryan) established

An Indo-European language — kin to Persian, Greek, Latin. Composed and transmitted ORALLY for centuries.

Evidence: contemporary text

Recorded by: the priestly (Brahmin) composers of the hymns

Shaped by: a PRIESTLY elite — this is the canon of one class

How we know: The language is known from the Vedas themselves — an extraordinary oral text preserved almost verbatim. But it is the voice of a ritual elite; the speech of everyone else (labourers, the conquered, women, the ‘dasa’) survives only through that filter. What we ‘know’ of the age is largely what the priests chose to chant.

ScriptNone at first — oral, then later Brahmi established

The Vedas were memorised, not written, for a very long time. Sanskrit had no script of its own.

Evidence: inference

Recorded by: the oral tradition (memory), written down much later

Shaped by: transmission by memory = control by the reciters

How we know: That the age was pre-literate is inferred from the absence of Vedic-era writing and the tradition's own oral method. Reliable — but note the whole record depended on WHO was permitted to memorise and recite it.

PeopleSteppe (Yamnaya) ancestry arrives established

Steppe pastoralist ancestry entered NW South Asia ~2000–1500 BCE — the same pulse that reshaped Bronze-Age Europe. The genetics are not disputed.

Evidence: peer reviewed

Recorded by: Narasimhan et al. 2019 (Science) — the Swat Valley time-transect

Shaped by: the GENETICS aren't shaped; the ‘Aryan’ NARRATIVE around them is fiercely contested

How we know: Here is the cleanest split in the whole timeline. That Steppe ancestry arrived ~2000–1500 BCE is peer-reviewed and NOT seriously disputed. What it MEANS — ‘Aryan invasion’, peaceful migration, or (some argue) an out-of-India origin — is a HISTORICAL interpretation, politically loaded, and genuinely contested. We hold the finding as established and flag the story as contested. ⚠

RulersTribal chiefdoms (janas), rajas debated

Pastoral, then settling agricultural societies along the Sapta-Sindhu and Ganga-Yamuna doab.

Evidence: contemporary text

Recorded by: the Vedas (the winners' hymns)

Shaped by: victors' hymns — battles are told from one side

How we know: The famous ‘Battle of the Ten Kings’ and the raja lineages come from the Rigveda itself — i.e. from the side that composed the hymn. Classic winner's account: the defeated (e.g. the ‘Dasa/Dasyu’) are described only by their enemies.

HeritageThe Vedas, the oral canon established

The Rigveda — among the oldest religious texts still in use; the Nāsadīya Sūkta (Hymn of Creation) sits here.

Evidence: contemporary text

Recorded by: the ṛṣis / priestly composers

Shaped by: the surviving canon = what one tradition preserved

How we know: The Vedas are a genuine, astonishingly well-preserved primary text OF THEIR KIND. But ‘heritage’ here is textual, not material — and it's the heritage the Vedic tradition kept. What rival or folk traditions of the age believed is largely lost.

  • Composition of the Rigveda
  • The ‘Aryan migration’ interpretation — genetics established, historical narrative CONTESTED
  • Shift east into the Gangetic plain

contested Parts of this era are debated; see each facet.

~600–350 BCE

The Mahajanapadas & the śramaṇa age

LanguagePrakrits (spoken) beside Sanskrit (formal)

The everyday Middle Indo-Aryan vernaculars — the real bridge to the modern languages — rise beside formal Sanskrit.

ScriptWriting re-emerges (Brahmi forming)

After the long post-Indus gap, writing returns; Brahmi is taking shape, ready for the Mauryan state.

PeopleANI/ASI blends forming

The two ancestral populations (Ancestral North & South Indians) mix in two directions; almost all South Asians today are a blend of them.

Rulers16 Mahajanapadas — Magadha rising debated

Republics (gaṇa-saṅghas) and kingdoms; Magadha grows dominant under the Haryanka/Nanda dynasties.

Evidence: later text

Recorded by: Buddhist & Jain canons + later Puranas

Shaped by: religious traditions writing their own founding era

How we know: The list of ‘16 great realms’ and their kings comes largely from Buddhist/Jain texts and later Puranic king-lists — written by the traditions that grew powerful, often centuries after. Real, but a later account, not contemporary record.

HeritageBuddhism & Jainism founded established

The Buddha and Mahavira teach in this age — a philosophical revolution that will shape the whole of Asia.

Evidence: later text

Recorded by: the Buddhist & Jain communities (canon written later)

Shaped by: the sanghas that survived preserved their own founders

How we know: That the Buddha and Mahavira lived and taught in this window is broadly accepted, but the biographies were written down generations later by devotees — reverent accounts, not neutral chronicle.

  • Rise of Magadha
  • Life of the Buddha & Mahavira
  • Second urbanisation of the Ganga plain

~322–185 BCE

The Mauryan Empire — Ashoka

LanguagePrakrit (state), Sanskrit, Greek & Aramaic on the frontier

The edicts speak in Prakrit at the core and switch to Greek/Aramaic on the Hellenistic edge — the multilingual reach of the empire.

ScriptBrahmi (core) · Kharoshthi (NW) · Greek · Aramaic established

The first securely readable Indian script. Brahmi in the core, Kharoshthi in the northwest — deciphered only in 1837 (Prinsep).

Evidence: primary

Recorded by: the Mauryan state (carved), read by Prinsep 1837

Shaped by: the state chose what to carve — but the OBJECT is unforgeable

How we know: The inscriptions are primary, physical and datable; the decipherment is well-established. Among the strongest evidence in the timeline — you can go touch the stone. (What it says is the state's message; that it exists and says it, is fact.)

PeopleSettled ANI/ASI populations established

The caste system begins to reduce inter-group mixing, ‘freezing’ ancestry proportions — itself readable in later DNA.

Evidence: peer reviewed

Recorded by: modern DNA (endogamy dated by later genomes)

Shaped by: none — a genetic signal, not a written claim

How we know: The onset of strong endogamy is dated genetically (Reich lab). It's an inference from modern/ancient DNA, not from any text — so it sidesteps the winner's-history filter entirely.

RulersChandragupta → Bindusara → Ashoka established

One of the largest ancient empires — from Afghanistan to Karnataka. We can VERIFY Ashoka's rule from his own edicts.

Evidence: contemporary text

Recorded by: Ashoka himself (the edicts) + later Buddhist chronicles

Shaped by: the KING himself — the edicts are self-portrait AND propaganda

How we know: Rare luxury: a ruler's OWN words survive, datable and empire-wide — so his reign is verifiable, not legend. BUT the edicts are also PR: ‘what he said’ (remorse, dhamma) is his chosen image, and must be kept apart from later hagiography (Ashokavadana) that turned him monster-to-saint. Strong evidence, read as self-portrait.

HeritageThe Edicts, the pillars, the Lion Capital (now India's emblem) established

Rock & pillar edicts across the empire; the Sarnath lion capital.

Evidence: primary

Recorded by: the Mauryan state (commissioned)

Shaped by: state-commissioned monuments — power made visible

How we know: Physical, standing, datable — the surest kind. Note that monumental heritage always over-represents the powerful: we have the emperor's pillars, not the villages.

  • The Kalinga war and Ashoka's remorse (Rock Edict 13)
  • State patronage of dhamma / Buddhism spreads
  • Brahmi carved empire-wide

~185 BCE – 550 CE

Post-Mauryan to the Gupta ‘classical’ age

LanguageClassical Sanskrit codified; Prakrits flourish; Old Tamil in the south

Pāṇini's grammar fixes Classical Sanskrit; in the Dravidian south, Old Tamil produces the Sangam literature — a parallel, unrelated tradition.

ScriptBrahmi diversifies → Gupta script; Tamil-Brahmi in the south

‘Late Brahmi’ (Gupta) is the hinge from which Devanagari and the northern scripts will grow; Tamil-Brahmi carries the Dravidian south.

PeopleThe settled blend of modern South Asians

Ancestry proportions much as today; regional communities distinct.

RulersShungas, Satavahanas, Kushanas, then the Guptas debated

The Gupta era is remembered as a ‘golden age’ of science, maths (the decimal/zero), art and literature.

Evidence: primary

Recorded by: coins, inscriptions + court poets (praśasti)

Shaped by: the very phrase ‘golden age’ is a court-and-later gloss

How we know: Gupta kings are well-attested by coins and inscriptions (primary). But ‘golden age’ is partly the Guptas' own court panegyric (e.g. the Allahabad praśasti of Samudragupta — literally a poem praising the king) and partly later nostalgia. The dynasty is fact; the glow is curated.

HeritageAjanta & Ellora, Sanchi, Sangam poetry, Kalidasa, Aryabhata established

Rock-cut cave temples, classical drama, and the mathematics that gave the world the decimal place-value system.

Evidence: primary

Recorded by: the works themselves (caves, texts, treatises)

Shaped by: elite patronage decided what got built & copied

How we know: Ajanta's paintings, Aryabhata's treatise, Kalidasa's plays survive as primary works. Rock-hard evidence they existed — though what survived is what wealthy patrons funded and later scribes chose to recopy.

  • Pāṇini's Sanskrit grammar
  • Sangam age of Tamil literature
  • Gupta ‘golden age’; Aryabhata; the concept of zero
