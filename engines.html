The engines

This atlas breaks India into pixels of money and a chain of command. It runs on a handful of engines — each one reads the same country through a different lens, and they mix. One number can belong to several at once: a flood-prone ward (Climate) that's a no-build coastal zone (Land Zoning) where a civic budget was still spent (Development) and an audit later flagged it (Corruption), reported the next morning (News).

The first engine wasn't ours. The Great Trigonometrical Survey of India spent seventy years (1802–1871) triangulating the subcontinent into measurable lines — the original apparatus that mapped this land in order to tax and govern it. We point the same instrument the other way: mapping the country to hold that apparatus accountable.

The rule every engine obeys

Sourced, or it's a gap. No engine invents a number. A figure is pinned to a government source or it's recorded as an explicit gap — you can audit every one of them on the Provenance ledger.

The Corruption engine is the one we wish we didn't need. It never calls a person corrupt. It only surfaces what an audit, a court, or an RTI reply has already established — a CAG paragraph, a court-ordered pay arrear, a prison over its sanctioned capacity — and lets the sourced record speak. Facts, never accusations.
