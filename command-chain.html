Governance protocol · administrative layer

How the chain of command actually works

Who appoints whom, and who answers to whom — from the President down to the village. The spine is the All-India Services (IAS / IPS): officers recruited by the Union but posted by the States. That split — "dual control" — is the structural fault line everything below hangs on. Colour shows the appointing authority.

District — the operational heart
District Magistrate / Collector (IAS)
Single most powerful district post. Revenue, magistracy, disaster, land, and nodal officer for most central schemes. Recruited by Union, posted by State.
Art. 312 · CrPC/BNSS
Critique — "steel cage"

One generalist officer gatekeeps dozens of unrelated domains — a colonial design built for revenue extraction, not service delivery.

How to read this

Red posts are the spine — recruited by the Union under Article 312 (All-India Services), then handed to States to post. That is "dual control": neither the Union nor the State fully owns the officer, which is exactly why both the appointing power (transfers) and the accountability gap (hard to dismiss) sit where they do.
