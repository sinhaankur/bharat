About, methodology & disclaimer

An independent civic-data project that maps how public money flows through India — from the Union, through States, to districts and local bodies — and who is accountable at each step. It republishes and structures information already in the public record.

Disclaimer

This is an independent, non-commercial research project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or representing the Government of India, any State Government, any ministry, the Finance Commission, the CAG, or any public body. It uses no government emblems and claims no official status. Figures are drawn from public government documents and are presented for transparency and public-interest research; they may contain errors or omissions and should be verified against the cited primary source before being relied upon. Commentary sections reflect opinion/analysis, clearly labelled as such, not statements of fact.

01What this project is

A district-by-district ledger of public money: what came in (central schemes, Finance Commission devolution, state & local grants, MPLADS), through which department, the intended outcome, and — where the public record allows — what actually happened (utilisation, unspent/lapsed funds, audit flags). It also decodes the governance protocol: who has constitutional authority over each subject, how the money is routed, who is appointed by whom, and what sanction/audit gates apply.

02The goal — informed decisions, not opinions

The point of this atlas is simple: help people make informed decisions about India from the record itself, not from spin. Whether you're a citizen, journalist, student, researcher or policymaker, you should be able to see what came in, who's accountable, and what the record shows — for your district, your state, and the whole country — and weigh any claim against the sourced numbers. Bias is treated as the sentiment of a section of people; the numbers are the spin-proof anchor. Understanding replaces assertion.

How it's built. It's a fully client-side, open project — no backend, no tracking of what you look at. Public datasets (Finance Commission reports, RBI's Handbook of Statistics on Indian States, Census 2011, MoSPI, Union Budget receipts, NCRB, MoEFCC, Natural Earth, open elevation DEMs, USGS, the Internet Archive) are processed offline into compact JSON/GeoJSON that the browser reads directly. The interfaces — the 2D map (Leaflet), the real-Earth 3D globe and district terrain (three.js), the reading room, and the one-screen hero app — all run in your browser from the same sourced data. Every figure is either cited to a public source or explicitly marked a gap (see the provenance ledger), so you can trace and reuse it. It's open source — inspect it, correct it, build on it.

03Sourcing rule — "PDF-cited or it's a gap"

The project's core rule: a figure ships only with a government source citation; otherwise it stays blank and is recorded as an explicit gap — never guessed, estimated, or filled in. Accountability claims are only as trustworthy as the document behind them, so every fact carries a source and a source tier:

  • Tier 1Government PDF — budget documents, CAG audit reports, Finance Commission reports, scheme guidelines. The gold standard.
  • Tier 2Government web (.gov.in / .nic.in) — official portals and district sites.
  • Tier 3Wikipedia — used for discovery only, visibly flagged as needing upgrade to a government source.
  • Tier 4News reports — lowest tier, flagged, used only where no official source yet exists.

04Coverage — depth vs. honesty

India has ~594 districts. A small number are hand-authored deep dives with real, PDF-cited money figures and named officials. The rest carry an honest baseline: the administrative model, the constitutional/fiscal structure of the schemes that apply, and the appointing authority of each post — but with monetary amounts left blank and listed as gaps.

This is deliberate. District-level utilisation, audit, and officer data are not published uniformly across all districts. Rather than fabricate precision, the project shows the structure everywhere and the figures only where a real government document backs them. An honest gap is preferred over an invented number.

05Fact vs. commentary

Two kinds of content are kept strictly separate. Documented data (money figures, allocations, official posts) is sourced and cited as above. Commentary and critique — for example, analysis of how the bureaucratic system functions or dysfunctions — is opinion and analysis, clearly labelled as critique, and is held to a lower bar than the cited figures. Where commentary draws on a public interview or article, it is attributed; it is not presented as established fact.

06Corrections & takedown

Accuracy matters more than being first. If you find an error, a misattributed figure, or believe something should be corrected or removed, the source is open and corrections are welcome.

Raise an issue or correction on the public repository: github.com/sinhaankur/india-fiscal-map/issues ↗. Every data point links back to its primary source on the Sources page — please check the cited document first.

07Licence & reuse

Underlying government data is public record. This project's structuring, code, and compilation are open on GitHub. If you reuse the data, cite the primary government source (linked throughout), not this site alone — this site is a convenience layer over the public record, not the authority for it.
