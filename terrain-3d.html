India's real relief

An interactive 3D surface of India built from open 30 m elevation data (SRTM / Terrarium terrain-RGB) — the actual topography: the Himalayan wall, the Deccan plateau, the flat Ganga plain, the Western Ghats dropping to the coast. Colour and height are metres above sea level. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom. Focus a region for finer detail, push the exaggeration to make the lows pop, and raise the sea to see which ground goes under first.

The 🌊 sea-level slider is a bathtub model. It floods every point below the chosen height — a fast "what sits low" view, not a hydrological projection. It ignores tides, storm surge, subsidence, embankments and drainage, and the +30 m / +70 m stops are geological ceilings shown for scale, not forecasts. The near-term stops (+1 m, +2 m) sit in the published IPCC AR6 ranges. On 30 m open DEM, a metre of sea rise is at the edge of what the elevation data can resolve — read it as direction, not a property-line.
Open DEM, not LIDAR. This relief uses open 30 m elevation (SRTM / ISRO CartoDEM class). India has no open nationwide LIDAR dataset — Indian LIDAR is project-specific and commercial, and the 2021 Geospatial Guidelines restrict high-resolution data. So sub-metre LIDAR relief isn't available to show; 30 m open DEM is the honest best.
Geography layer · flood simulation

Water over Kolkata's real terrain

A GPU fluid simulation (lattice-Boltzmann, free surface) of a flood surge from the Hooghly edge across Kolkata district's actual elevation — the same open DEM as the relief above (~33 m/pixel for this scene). The white surface is the district's terrain; blue is water.

Top-down view — the flood spreading across the district footprint
Illustrative physics, not a forecast. Real fluid dynamics on real open-DEM terrain, but not a calibrated hydrological model: the vertical scale is exaggerated ~33× (Kolkata's true relief is only ~0–19 m across 14 km — unexaggerated it would be a flat sheet), the surge volume is chosen for legibility, and drainage/pumping isn't modelled. For the sourced flood-risk record per district, use the main map's Flood and Low-lying % facets — the latter is computed from this same open DEM for all 594 districts. And for ANY district, the interactive 3D flood explorer ↗ lets you raise the water yourself over its real terrain.

Terrain: AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM-derived, open), Kolkata district bbox 88.271–88.407 E, 22.495–22.630 N. Simulated offline with FluidX3D (© Dr. Moritz Lehmann, free for non-commercial use) — D3Q19 LBM, free-surface, 39 M cells on an Apple M1 Max. Only the rendered video ships to your browser.

Elevation tiles: AWS Terrain Tiles (Terrarium, open), encoding SRTM / NASADEM / other open sources. Rendered client-side with three.js — no data is stored or redistributed. Height = decoded metres above sea level × the exaggeration slider. For the district-level constraint view, see India extruded by constraint ↗; for the flat map with a hillshade toggle, the main map.
