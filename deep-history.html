Who we are, in DNA

What ancient DNA reveals about who migrated, mixed and was replaced across the subcontinent — from Neanderthals to the Steppe and the Indus genome.

How we knowHeld strictly to peer-reviewed ancient-DNA science (Reich lab; Narasimhan & Shinde 2019). A genetic finding (established) is kept separate from a historical interpretation (the 'Aryan migration' debate is flagged contested).

Finding first, interpretation second

Eight shifts, in order

~65,000+ years ago · Indian subcontinent

AASI — the deep substrate of South Asia

A deeply divergent hunter-gatherer lineage (AASI), distantly related to the Andamanese and other early out-of-Africa peoples, formed the ancient population substrate of the subcontinent — the layer everything later mixed into.

Interpretation, kept apart: AASI is not sampled directly (no ancient AASI genome yet) but is inferred as a source in every model of South Asian ancestry.

established Narasimhan et al. 2019, Science

~60,000–40,000 years ago · Eurasia (out-of-Africa)

Homo sapiens × Neanderthal admixture (and Neanderthal disappearance)

As modern humans left Africa they interbred with Neanderthals; every non-African person alive carries ~1.5–2.1% Neanderthal DNA. Neanderthals then vanished from the record ~40,000 years ago.

Interpretation, kept apart: Whether sapiens actively 'killed off' Neanderthals or outcompeted/absorbed them is debated — the DNA proves interbreeding and eventual replacement, but NOT a specific massacre. This is the user's 'sapiens taking over Neanderthals' point, stated at the level the evidence supports: replacement + admixture, mechanism uncertain.

established Green et al. 2010, Science; subsequent Neanderthal-genome work

~7000–5000 years ago · NW South Asia / Iranian plateau

Iranian-farmer-related ancestry enters South Asia

Ancestry related to Neolithic Iranian-plateau farmers mixed with AASI to form the people of the Indus Valley Civilisation. The single sequenced IVC individual (Rakhigarhi) was exactly this mix — with NO Steppe and NO Anatolian-farmer ancestry.

Interpretation, kept apart: This ties living South Asians directly to the IVC population. The absence of Steppe ancestry in the IVC individual is a key data point in the Aryan-migration debate below.

established Shinde et al. 2019, Cell (Rakhigarhi genome)

individual dated ~2800–2300 BCE (sequenced 2019) · Haryana (Ghaggar-Hakra plain)

Rakhigarhi genome ties living South Asians to the IVC

The first (and so far only) sequenced Indus Valley genome, from a woman buried at Rakhigarhi, carried Iranian-related + AASI ancestry and NO Steppe or Anatolian-farmer ancestry — direct genetic continuity from the IVC to modern South Asians.

Interpretation, kept apart: The authors caution that this is a single individual, limiting firm conclusions. Links to the same Rakhigarhi site in the heritage atlas.

established Shinde et al. 2019, Cell

~4000–3500 years ago (~2000–1500 BCE) · Central Asia → NW South Asia (Swat Valley)

Steppe (Yamnaya) pastoralist ancestry spreads into South Asia

Steppe (Yamnaya-related) ancestry — the same profile seen spreading into Bronze-Age Europe — reached South Asia in the first half of the 2nd millennium BCE, contributing up to ~30% of the ancestry of some modern groups. Indo-European-speaking groups carry more of it than Dravidian-speaking groups.

Interpretation, kept apart: The Reich-lab authors read this as the movement that likely spread Indo-Iranian languages into the subcontinent. THIS is the scientific basis of the 'Aryan migration' picture — see the contested entry.

established Narasimhan et al. 2019, Science (Swat Valley time-transect)

interpretation of the ~2000-1500 BCE data · Indian subcontinent

The 'Aryan migration vs. indigenous origin' interpretation

The GENETICS are not seriously disputed: Steppe ancestry entered South Asia ~2000-1500 BCE. What is contested is the HISTORICAL narrative attached to it.

Interpretation, kept apart: Mainstream genetics/linguistics reads the Steppe influx as an inward MIGRATION that spread Indo-Aryan languages (a migration, not necessarily a violent 'invasion'). Some Indian scholars/commentators dispute the direction, timing, or the 'Aryan' label, or argue for an indigenous / Out-of-India origin. We present the genetic finding as established and the invasion/indigenous framing as contested — deliberately NOT taking a side beyond the data.

debated Narasimhan et al. 2019, Science; and the surrounding scholarly debate

~4000–2000 years ago · Indian subcontinent

Formation of the two ancestral populations (ANI & ASI)

The IVC-derived population mixed in two directions: southeast (forming the Ancestral South Indians, ASI) and with Steppe-descended groups (forming the Ancestral North Indians, ANI). Almost all South Asians today are a blend of ANI and ASI, whose common thread is the IVC-like ancestry.

Interpretation, kept apart: Later the caste system reduced inter-group mixing, 'freezing' distinct ANI/ASI proportions across communities — itself readable in the DNA.

established Reich et al.; Narasimhan et al. 2019

the through-line of the whole record · Subcontinent → Eurasia

How related everyone is — the borders are younger than the blood

Almost every living South Asian is a blend of the SAME three ancient sources (AASI + Iranian-farmer-related + Steppe), differing only in proportion. The very same Yamnaya/Steppe ancestry that entered South Asia also spread into Bronze-Age Europe — so North Indians and Europeans literally share a documented ancestral pulse. There is no genetically 'pure' or separate people here; the modern national and communal lines are far younger than the shared ancestry beneath them.

Interpretation, kept apart: This is the atlas's honest point: a DNA map shows how connected people are, not how divided. Borders drawn by 20th-century politics, and identities hardened by religion or nation, sit on top of a population that is deeply intermixed. The science does not endorse any 'us vs them' — it dissolves it. (What it does NOT do is license claims about any group being superior, indigenous-and-therefore-owning, or foreign-and-therefore-lesser — descent is not desert.)

established Narasimhan et al. 2019, Science (shared Yamnaya ancestry in Europe & South Asia; ANI/ASI as blends of common sources)
