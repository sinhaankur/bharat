Eight colours, each from somewhere

Indian Yellow

A luminous golden lake pigment (piuri / peori / gogili) reportedly made at Monghyr — modern Munger, Bihar — from the concentrated urine of cows fed only mango leaves and water. Documented by the Indian civil servant T. N. Mukharji in 1883 at the request of Kew's Sir Joseph Hooker; long doubted, then largely confirmed by 21st-century chemistry identifying euxanthate salts.

#c9a227 · Munger (Monghyr), Bihar

rajput, gupta, mauryan

Indigo · Nīla

The blue dye of Indigofera tinctoria — called nīla ("dark blue") in India, where its use is attested from the Indus Valley (Mohenjo-daro / Lothal, c. 2500–2000 BCE). The English word 'indigo' comes from Greek indikón, 'from India'. India dominated the world indigo trade for two millennia; it was called 'blue gold'.

#274a8c · Indus Valley; later Bengal & Bihar plantations

gupta

Ultramarine · Lapis lazuli

The deepest blue of the Ajanta murals, ground from lapis lazuli. Raman-spectroscopy provenance work on Ajanta pigment samples points to the stone being imported from the Sar-e-Sang mines of Badakhshan, Afghanistan — mined for over 6,000 years — reaching the Deccan by trade.

#2a4a7a · Ajanta Caves, Maharashtra (stone from Badakhshan)

chola, rajput, mauryan

Vermilion · Kumkum / Sindūra

The sacred red of the temple and the miniature — historically vermilion, the brilliant scarlet of ground cinnabar (mercury sulphide) and its synthetic twin. In ritual use as kumkum / sindūra it marks the deity, the threshold and the married woman; the same red fills the Ajanta palette as iron-oxide.

#b3341f · Temple towns of the Tamil country; pan-Indian ritual use

mauryan

Chunar buff · Mauryan sandstone

The buff / tan sandstone of Chunar, quarried on the Ganga near Varanasi, from which the Ashokan pillars were cut and given the famous mirror-like 'Mauryan polish'. The paper of the Mauryan system is this stone in sunlight.

#e9ddc7 · Chunar, Uttar Pradesh

gupta

Mathura red sandstone

The mottled red-and-cream sandstone of the Mathura region, the signature medium of Kushan and Gupta sculpture — the great Gupta Buddha and Vishnu figures were carved from it. Its warm red is the ground of the Gupta 'soul' register.

#c8664a · Mathura, Uttar Pradesh

chola

Chola bronze · patina

The lost-wax cast bronze of the Chola masters — the Nataraja and the processional icons — an alloy that ages from warm metal to a green-brown verdigris patina. The system's ink and success-green are that metal and its ageing.

#7d5a2e · Thanjavur / Kaveri delta, Tamil Nadu

rajput

Jaisalmer golden sandstone

The honey-gold yellow sandstone of Jaisalmer, so pervasive that the fort and town are called the 'Golden City'. The carved havelis and the living fort are cut from it; it is the sunlit ground of the Rajput register.

#e7c98a · Jaisalmer, Rajasthan
