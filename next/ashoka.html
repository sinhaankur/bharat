Kalinga, and the remorse

When King Beloved-of-the-Gods, Priyadarsin, had been anointed eight years, (the country of) the Kalingas was conquered by (him). One hundred and fifty thousand in number were the men who were deported thence, one hundred thousand in number were those who were slain there, and many times as many those who died.Major Rock Edict 13 — the Kalinga war and the ruler's remorse
After that, now that (the country of) the Kalingas has been taken, Beloved-of-the-Gods (is devoted) to a zealous study of morality (Dharma), to the love of morality, and to the instruction (of people) in morality. This is the repentance of Beloved-of-the-Gods on account of his conquest of (the country of) the Kalingas.trans. E. Hultzsch, 'Inscriptions of Asoka' (1925, public domain)

The rarest thing in the ancient record: a victorious emperor publishing his own regret at the human cost of his victory, and pledging a change of policy — carved not once but repeated across the empire. It is the strongest single reason the edicts are read as more than boast.

19 edicts

What the edicts say

EdictThemeSummary
Major Rock 1No animal slaughter / end of the feast-killBans animal sacrifice and festive gatherings; Ashoka records cutting the daily kill in the royal kitchen from many thousands to two peacocks and a deer, and aims for none.
Major Rock 2Welfare — medicine, wells, treesMedical care for humans AND animals, medicinal herbs planted, wells dug and shade-trees set along roads — even in the lands of neighbouring kings (the Cholas, Pandyas, and 'Antiyoka the Greek king').
Major Rock 3Officers tour every 5 yearsAdministrative order that officials tour the realm every five years to teach dhamma alongside their duties: governance as moral instruction.
Major Rock 4Dhamma replaces the war-drumWhere kings once sounded the war-drum (bherī-ghosa), Ashoka now sounds the 'drum of dhamma' (dhamma-ghosa) — the reign's programme in one image.
Major Rock 5Dhamma-mahāmātras (officers of morality)Creates a new cadre of 'dhamma officers' to look after the welfare and fair treatment of all sects, prisoners, the aged, and border peoples.
Major Rock 6The king always on dutyAshoka orders that reports reach him at ALL times, wherever he is — eating, in the harem, walking — because 'I am never content with my exertion': an ideology of the ever-available ruler.
Major Rock 7All sects may dwell everywhereWishes that people of ALL religious sects may live in all places, seeking self-control and purity of heart — a charter of coexistence.
Major Rock 8Tours of dhamma replace pleasure-toursInstead of royal hunting/pleasure tours (vihāra-yātrā), Ashoka now makes 'dhamma tours' (dhamma-yātrā) — visiting the people, elders and ascetics.
Major Rock 9True ceremony is conduct, not ritualDownplays showy rituals (especially those women perform); the ceremony that bears fruit is right conduct toward servants, elders, animals and ascetics.
Major Rock 10Fame is worthless without dhammaAshoka rejects glory and fame as ends in themselves; the only worthwhile renown is that people follow dhamma.
Major Rock 11The gift of dhamma is the best giftThe 'gift of dhamma' — right relations among people — surpasses every material gift, and spreads without limit.
Major Rock 12Honour other sects — restrain your own praiseA striking plea for religious restraint: honour other sects, and do NOT over-praise your own or disparage others, 'for whoever praises his own sect... harms his own sect'.
Major Rock 13The Kalinga war & remorseThe famous edict: the conquest of Kalinga, its terrible toll, Ashoka's remorse, and his turn from conquest-by-arms to 'conquest by dhamma' — even naming the Hellenistic kings (Antiochus, Ptolemy, Antigonus, Magas, Alexander) among whom he claims dhamma has spread.
Major Rock 14Why these edicts were writtenA closing note that the edicts exist in short, medium and long forms, engraved across the vast empire so all may see — the edicts explaining themselves.
Major Pillar IRule by dhammaProtection through dhamma, self-examination, and the fear of sin as the guard of good government.
Major Pillar VProtected species / animal welfare lawA remarkable conservation LAW: lists dozens of protected animals and birds, bans castration and branding on certain days, and forbids killing on numerous holy days — arguably the world's earliest wildlife-protection statute.
Major Pillar VIIThe reign reviewedAshoka's retrospective: the dhamma-officers, the planted trees and wells, and his hope that his sons and great-grandsons will continue the work 'as long as the moon and sun endure'.
Separate (Kalinga) 1–2Governing the conqueredAt Dhauli & Jaugada in Kalinga: officers told to treat ALL subjects as the king's own children, to avoid harshness and wrongful imprisonment — the administrative face of the post-war turn.
Minor Pillar —Tax relief at the Buddha's birthplaceAt Lumbini: Ashoka, on visiting the Buddha's birthplace, exempts the village from the bali tax and fixes its land-revenue share at one-eighth — a concrete, dated act of taxation policy carved in stone.
