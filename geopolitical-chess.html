Geopolitical Chess

⚠ This whole page is a framing

How this works

The idea. The US dollar is treated as the board every country must play on; each country is a piece. Real chess has exactly two sides — the real world doesn't. The number of poles (power centres) has flipped over the last century: multipolar empires → bipolar (US vs USSR) → a unipolar US moment → back to multipolar today. Pieces change colour by which pole they belong to in the selected year, and countries visibly switch sides (e.g. Iran was a US ally under the Shah, then a US adversary after 1979).

What you can do

  • Drag the era timeline (or click a year) to replay how the board changed — pieces re-colour into that year's poles.
  • Watch the flip callout under the board: it names who switched allegiance between years (hover the ℹ for the sourced story).
  • Hover a piece for its role & pole; hover a line for the tie between two players.
  • Click a piece (or a legend/flip name) to jump to that country's full card.
  • Toggle the entanglement lines to read the board clean or wired.
  • Scroll down for each player's ruling party, power nexus, best/worst outcome, the entanglements, and the moves timeline (history → live 2026).

How to read it honestly

  • Numbers are sourced (see below); the chess reading — poles, moves, "best/worst", client-state calls — is Framing editorial interpretation, tagged as such.
  • The dominance meter ("one-power concentration") is a framing gauge, not a single official statistic.
  • Earlier eras show fewer active poles/players because many modern states weren't independent powers yet — that's honest, not missing data.
  • It's a model, not a verdict — argue with it.
