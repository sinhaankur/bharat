Commentary, labelled

Two sides of the ledger

Strengths on record

  • Strategic gateway to Northeast and to Bangladesh/Bhutan trade.
  • Diverse economy: tea, jute, manufacturing in Haldia, IT in Salt Lake/Rajarhat.
  • FC15 share rose slightly — recognising population needs over economic dynamism.

Strains on record

  • Deindustrialisation since the 1970s — Kolkata's manufacturing base hollowed out.
  • Heavy debt burden; high revenue deficit constrains capital spending.
  • Political friction with Center periodically delays GST compensation and central scheme funds.

These summaries are the atlas's own analysis, kept apart from the sourced figures above.

6 system notes

Notes in the ledger

Kolkata · structural dysfunction

Kolkata

Kolkata has no conventional District Magistrate/Collector. Magisterial powers vest in the Commissioner of Police (one of the oldest such posts in British India); a limited Collector post handles stamp revenue/registration; service delivery is run by the Kolkata Municipal Corporation. A standard 'who is the DM responsible for this money' question therefore has no single answer here.

source T3

Kolkata · delivery dysfunction

Kolkata

MPLADS delivery in Kolkata's metro seats is recommendation-heavy but completion-poor: Kolkata Uttar completed 3 of 36 works, Kolkata Dakshin 0 of 73 (as on 2026-01-06). A WB-wide review found dense metropolitan seats show limited tangible outcomes versus rural constituencies — money flows in on paper but converts to far less on the ground.

source T4

Kolkata · structural

Kolkata

Fiscal dependence: ~52% of Kolkata Municipal Corporation's ₹5,525 cr receipts (BE 2025-26) is Government Grant-in-Aid from Central + State governments (₹2,897 cr), versus ₹2,628 cr raised from its own sources. More than half of the city's civic money is transferred down rather than locally generated — and the budget still runs a deficit (expenditure ₹5,639.56 cr).

source T1

Birbhum · fund freeze dysfunction

Birbhum

MGNREGS — the rural job-guarantee scheme — was FROZEN for West Bengal (and thus Birbhum) for nearly four years. The Centre stopped fund release from 9 March 2022 under Section 27 of the MGNREGA Act, citing non-compliance/corruption. Work resumed only 1 June 2026 after Supreme Court intervention. This is the opposite dysfunction to Kolkata's: not 'money came and wasn't spent', but money WITHHELD from the top — wages went unpaid for months.

source T4

Birbhum · structural

Birbhum

Pending MGNREGS dues to West Bengal stood at ₹3,038 cr (Centre's figure, 2025 Rajya Sabha) — the highest of any state; the state claims ₹18,000 cr across MGNREGS+PMAY-G. A standard rural district like Birbhum, MGNREGS-dependent, bore the brunt: assets unbuilt, migrants displaced.

source T4

Birbhum · delivery contrast

Birbhum

Delivery contrast: Bolpur (the Birbhum-area Lok Sabha seat) is West Bengal's TOP MPLADS performer — MP Asit Kumar Mal recommended 189 works, completed 67, and spent ₹14.27 cr (highest of all 42 WB MPs), versus Kolkata's metro seats completing 3/36 and 0/73. Rural delivery outpaced the metros.

source T4
