The story — how we designed it

inspired-by · origins · decisions

What it's inspired from

The Rajput register is drawn from the desert courts of Rajasthan (c. 8th–18th c.) — the golden-sandstone forts of Jaisalmer and Jodhpur, the mirror-inlaid sheesh mahal, and above all Rajput and Mewar miniature painting: vivid indigo, vermilion, malachite green and gold leaf on cream wasli paper, framed by the arched jharokha window.

What we know of its origins

Rajput kingdoms held the arid northwest through fort-cities and a courtly painting tradition that recorded epics, ragas and royal life in jewel-like pages. The blue of those pages is indigo — nīla, India's 'blue gold', cultivated here from antiquity and later the cause of the Champaran revolt; the gold is often the very Indian Yellow made downriver in Munger. The palette is read from surviving folios and haveli walls.

The design decisions that followed

From that heritage we made concrete choices, each traceable to a source:

  • Wasli as paper. The ground is cream miniature paper (--background) — the page you paint on.
  • Vermilion as action; indigo as the second voice. The two dominant miniature pigments become the two loudest UI roles.
  • Gold-leaf hairline. Cards carry a gold-leaf-to-indigo edge — the illuminated border of a folio.
  • Arched, crisp corner. Radius is a 5px jharokha softness — ornamented but sharp, never plush.
  • Sheesh-mahal at night. The dark register is deep indigo with gold and mirror glints — the mirror hall by lamplight.

Every colour below carries its own origin story — click any swatch marked “story” to see where the pigment came from and the reference behind it. A colour here is a place with a history, not a decoration.

Standards & legacy

how the empire held the line

The Rajput courts kept their standard through the atelier — the royal karkhana where master painters trained pupils to a house hand. A legacy is not an accident; it is a standard, maintained, workshop to workshop. Here is how the courts held the line.

The gharana / atelier

A court's paintings share a recognisable hand because they came from one atelier — a lineage (gharānā) of masters and pupils. Mewar, Kishangarh, Bundi each held a distinct, teachable style.

→ our one shared stylesheet: a single source every page is trained on.

A fixed making-order

Miniatures followed a set process — wasli paper, under-drawing, burnished ground, mineral pigment in order, gold last — so results were consistent across many hands.

→ our atomic tiers: tokens → atoms → molecules, a fixed order of composition.

A canon of forms

Ragamala and epic sets repeated an agreed iconographic vocabulary — postures, colour codes, framing (the jharokha) — a visual grammar every painter knew.

→ our component library: an agreed vocabulary of reusable parts.

Sourced: the Rajput / Rajasthani painting tradition and its court ateliers (Mewar, Kishangarh, Bundi–Kota); miniature technique on wasli. Read from surviving folios and workshop practice — Rajput painting ↗ · forts & courts

The method

how to read this page

Why atomic, and how we decide the tier

Atomic Design (Brad Frost) borrows a chemistry metaphor: interfaces are built from atoms that combine into molecules, which assemble into organisms, laid into templates. We add a tier 0 · Tokens underneath — the raw values (colour, type, space, elevation) that everything else is made of. The point isn't taxonomy for its own sake; it's reuse: fix a token once and the whole system reskins — which is exactly how one scaffold serves Mauryan, Gupta, and a design system per state.

The decision rule we use on every specimen:

Atom — cannot be broken down and still do its job. A <button>, an input, a badge, a rule, the spinner. It has no meaning of its own beyond its form. Molecule — a small, reusable group of atoms that together do one job: a label + input + error is a field; a set of atoms bonded to a single purpose. If removing one atom breaks the purpose, it's a molecule, not a coincidental grouping. Organism — a distinct, self-contained section: a table, a dialog, a nav bar. It can stand alone on a page. Template — the page-level skeleton where organisms are placed; content-agnostic layout.

Each specimen below is tagged with its tier and — where the call is interesting — a line on why.

Tokens

the raw material

Why tokens sit below the atoms

An atom is a thing (a button); a token is a value (the gold it's painted with). Tokens are the only place a hex, a font, or a pixel is allowed to be literal. Everything upward references them, so a skin is nothing more than a different set of token values on the same atoms. Never hard-code — reference the variable. Rajput values are drawn from miniature painting and fort architecture: cream wasli paper, Jaisalmer sandstone, court indigo, and vermilion. The action colour is miniature vermilion; corners take a 5px jharokha-arch softness — ornamented but crisp.

Colour

token

Wasli & miniature pigment. Cream paper, sepia ink, vermilion action, indigo as the secondary hue, gold-leaf highlights.

Type scale

token

One modular scale. Display serif for headlines, sans for body, mono for labels & data. Use the step, never an ad-hoc pixel. The miniature was built in a fixed making-order — wasli, under-drawing, burnished ground, mineral pigment, gold last. This type ramp is that discipline: measured steps in sequence, never ad-hoc.

Spacing · radius · elevation

token

A 4px rhythm; a tight radius (the Mauryan register drops corners to an incised 2px); one shadow ladder. Consistency here is what makes layouts feel calm. The corner takes a 5px jharokha arch — the cusped curve of the fort's oriel window. Ornamented but crisp: a Rajput line is painted with a squirrel-hair brush, sharp, never plush.

Atoms

indivisible primitives

Why these are atoms

Each thing here does one job and can't be split without losing it. A button is still a button with nothing inside but a word; a badge is a coloured word; a rule is a line. They carry no content logic — you drop them anywhere. If you find yourself giving an "atom" a heading and a body and a control, it stopped being an atom two additions ago.

Buttons

atom

The primary action wears the house gold; dark = stone-ink; ghost = incised outline. The action colour is miniature vermilion — cinnabar, the loudest pigment on the wasli page, saved for the turban, the flag, the one thing the eye must go to first. A button is that vermilion.

Text input · textarea · select

atom

Native form elements, house-skinned, with full state coverage: rest, hover, focus, disabled, invalid. An empty field is a sheet of burnished wasli awaiting the brush; the focus ring is the under-drawing's first charcoal line. The invalid state borrows the vermilion of a correction laid over the fault.

Checkbox · radio · switch

atom

Custom visuals over a real native input, so keyboard and screen-reader semantics stay free. A choice set down in mineral pigment — the miniaturist's committed stroke, made once on paper that will not forgive. The switch is a haveli lamp lit or dark; the checkbox, the gold dot that says done.

Tokens: --accent --muted --border-stronga11y: the hidden native input is the source of truth; focus ring on the custom box.

Molecules

atoms bonded to one job

Why these are molecules, not atoms

Each is several atoms bonded so tightly that the group is the reusable unit. A field is a label + input + help/error — pull the label off and the input loses its accessible name; the bond is the point. A segmented control is buttons that only mean something as a mutually-exclusive set. The test: if the atoms are only meaningful together, and you'd reuse them together, it's a molecule. A molecule still isn't a whole page section — that's the next tier up.

Field (label + control + help/error)

molecule

The smallest genuinely useful form unit — and the clearest atom-vs-molecule example on the page. Label, field and gloss bound as one — like a miniature: the painted scene, the illuminated border that holds it, and the Devanagari couplet inscribed above. The page is the whole.

Segmented control · tabs · pagination

molecule

Sets of atoms that only mean anything as a group — one choice out of several. A Ragamala set is thirty-six moods, each its own folio, turned one at a time. The segmented control is that album — distinct pages, one binding, one lifted to view at a time.

Organisms

standalone sections

Why these are organisms

An organism is a distinct, self-sufficient part of an interface — you could point at it on a page and name it: "the data table," "the dialog," "the card grid," "the toolbar." It composes molecules and atoms, owns a chunk of layout, and often owns behaviour. The line from molecule to organism: a molecule is a control; an organism is a place.

Templates & patterns

where organisms live

Why templates are the top tier

A template is the page-level skeleton: it arranges organisms into a layout without caring what content fills them. It's the difference between "a card grid" (organism) and "the front-door layout: hero, then a start-here row, then topic sections" (template). Our real templates already ship as pages — home.html, the article layout, the atlas shell. This tier is the bridge from the system to the product.
