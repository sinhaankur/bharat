India, extruded by constraint

Every state rises in proportion to the physical constraints on development that we already source in the geography layer — coastal (CRZ applies), flood-prone (CWC/NDMA), and terrain difficulty. The Himalayan flood belt stands tallest; the flat plains sit low. Colour is the constraint class. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.

Honest gap — not a “no government land” model. You asked for a civilian-area-only version excluding government land. India has no open cadastral ownership layer to separate civilian from government parcels (land records are per-state, mostly not machine-readable), so this model includes all land. That exclusion is recorded, not silently applied — see india-glb-meta.json.

What the height means. It is a transparent, additive index of already-sourced constraint flags (base + coastal + flood-prone + terrain), not a development-outcome or “hindrance score.” The point is to see where the land itself pushes back on building — then read that beside the money flow on the map. Model: open glTF 2.0 (india.glb), built from india-states.geojson by build_india_glb.py. Sources: MoEFCC CRZ Notification 2019, CWC/NDMA flood lists, India-WRIS, standard physiography. Comparative, no composite verdict.
