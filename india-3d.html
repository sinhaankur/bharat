How real is this? — every layer, and where reality runs out

This is a real simulation of the real Earth — built on measured, open data — and it says so plainly where the data runs out. Three honesty tiers:

✅ Real — measured data, drawn as it is

The Earth itself (NASA-derived day/night/cloud textures on a true sphere) plus world cities and world rivers on every continent (Natural Earth) — mapped worldwide, not just India. All 594 districts at their real Census-2011 centroids, sized by real population/literacy. India's own rivers, roads, railways, lakes, coastlines in depth (Natural Earth). CRZ-2019 lines, eco-sensitive zones, ponds (real MoEFCC / india-geodata polygons). Ocean-floor bathymetry (real isobaths). 35,941 Gandhinagar buildings with real height on the fly-down, and real bridges anywhere (live OpenStreetMap, on the drop-to-street view). Annual PM2.5 air quality. And in the impact sim, which land floods is decided by real DEM elevation.

🟡 Real timing / structure — illustrative magnitude (stated on-page)

Seasonal river flow follows India's real IMD monsoon calendar, but the flow amount is illustrative of the cycle, not gauge discharge. The impact simulator uses real elevation but a uniform water line, not wave physics. The sea-level & ocean-depth shells are exaggerated at globe scale so a few metres are visible. The shape is real; the scale is illustrative — and each note below spells out exactly which.

🟥 Declared gaps — honestly not shown, never faked

43 districts show grey — no confident Census match, so no invented number. Only 13 districts light up in "ledger depth" mode; the other 581 are placed and named, not fabricated to Kolkata depth (structure scaled, not figures). Agriculture zones, pre-2000 zoning history, per-river discharge, and the full court-case universe are declared gaps — India has no open dataset for them, so they're absent, not guessed.

The rule across the whole atlas: open-sourced-or-a-gap, never faked. Where reality is available it's here as measured; where it isn't, the globe tells you — that's what makes it trustworthy, not just detailed. Each layer's own note is below.

The Impact simulator is a real-elevation bathtub, not a physics forecast. Pick an event — sea-level rise, cyclone surge, tsunami, or an asteroid ocean-impact wave — and the water rises to that event's characteristic height. Which land goes under is real: it's the districts whose actual DEM elevation sits below the line (from the same open elevation data as 3D topography, all 594 districts). The event heights are representative reference values and it floods everything below them uniformly — it ignores wave direction, run-up, drainage and timing. The classic fluid simulation over Kolkata's real terrain is the physics version, offline, for one district.
Land disputes are the sourced cases, with their pending age. The ⚖️ layer stands a column over each documented land/water court case — taller and redder the longer it's been pending (filing/order year → now). These are the cases the atlas has sourced (NGT / High Court / Supreme Court orders, each with a citation). The full universe of India's land litigation lives in the eCourts / NGT portals — public (the govt does post it) but not a bulk-downloadable dataset, so "all cases" is a declared gap, grown case-by-case as each is sourced, never scraped-and-guessed.
The Study view overlays three real forces — for eyeballing, not a causal claim. The ⚡ Study preset (or the 🔴 Earthquakes toggle) shows 2,000 real USGS earthquake epicenters (M4.5+, 2000–2025, India region — size = magnitude, colour = depth) together with the monsoon-swollen rivers and the flood/sea water. It lets you see where the Himalayan seismic belt, the big river systems and the monsoon coincide — the collision-zone geography that shapes all three. It is an overlay for observation, not a claim that one causes another; real correlation (e.g. reservoir-induced seismicity, monsoon-loading of faults) needs proper analysis, and is a genuine research question, not asserted here. Quakes: USGS (public domain); monsoon timing: IMD; water: real DEM/flood zones.
The 594 districts are placed for real, coloured only where sourced. Every district sits at its true Census-2011 centroid. Coloured/sized by population or literacy, that's real Census data — districts we couldn't confidently match show grey (a gap), not an invented number. The "sourced ledger depth" mode lights up only the 13 districts with a full money-ledger (Kolkata-depth); the other 581 are named and placed, not fabricated to that depth — the atlas scales structure, not figures. Hover any marker for its name + value; click it to open that district's page.
Air quality is annual-average, not live. The 😷 layer stands a coloured column over each major city — taller and redder the dirtier the air — from annual-mean PM2.5 (IQAir World Air Quality Report / CPCB). India routinely holds many of the world's most-polluted cities; the WHO annual guideline is 5 µg/m³, so nearly every city shown is many times over (Delhi ≈ 90+). These are city-level yearly averages — not real-time and not per-station. Live AQI is CPCB / WAQI (keyed API), a separate opt-in.
Zones are the sourced flags, honestly limited. The Zones selector shows the zone types the atlas actually sources — CRZ (MoEFCC 2019, 73 coastal districts), flood-hazard (CWC/NDMA, 87), plus unsafe / palaeochannel / encroachment zones — placed at district centroids (approximate, not the zone-map polygon). Agriculture zones and zoning changes 1900→now are declared gaps: India has no open, nationwide zoning-map or land-use-history dataset to draw them from, so they aren't shown rather than invented.
Seasonal river flow follows the real monsoon calendar, illustratively. Drag the Month slider and the rivers swell through the south-west monsoon (Jun–Sep, peaking Aug–Sep) and thin in the dry season — the timing is India's actual monsoon onset/withdrawal (IMD), but the flow magnitude is illustrative of the cycle, not gauge discharge. Per-river monthly discharge (CWC / India-WRIS) is a separate, largely restricted dataset — a declared gap, not faked here.
The boundary is India's full claimed map. States use india-states.geojson, which extends to ~37° N and includes PoK (Gilgit-Baltistan) and Aksai Chin — the official Survey-of-India depiction, not the de-facto line-of-control. District-level geometry (Census-2011) stops short of the northern claimed area, so this states view is the correct one for the full boundary. Rivers are Natural Earth centre-lines (public domain), clipped to India — representative courses, not survey-grade channels.
